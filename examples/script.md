# Why your tabletop cupped (Northwind Woodshop, draft 2)

<!-- Structure: problem, causes, fix. Package: "Why Your Tabletop Cupped (and 3 Fixes That Work)", thumbnail "3 FIXES" on a cupped walnut top. -->

## Hook
[close-up: a straightedge rocking on a cupped walnut top]
Two weeks after glue-up, this walnut top rocked on the bench. Why does a flat board turn into a shallow bowl?
By the end you will know the three causes, and the one test that tells you which one you have.

## Minimum world
[diagram: a board end-on, growth rings drawn]
Wood takes on water from the air and lets it go again. So a board is never finished moving.
It moves most across the grain, and almost nothing along it.
A flatsawn walnut board can change width by about one percent between a dry winter and a humid summer [c1].
On a top 80 centimetres wide, that is close to a centimetre of movement.
[b-roll: moisture meter on the top]

## Cause one: one face drier than the other
If one face loses water faster than the other, that face shrinks first. The board cups toward the dry face.
That is what happened here. The top sat on a cold concrete floor, finished on one side only [c2].
[payoff]
So the first fix is free: finish both faces, and store the top flat with air on both sides.

## Cause two: the wood was not ready
Next, the boards themselves.
Lumber that came out of a kiln last week and went straight into a humid garage was never at the moisture level of the room it lives in, and it will keep adjusting for weeks after you glue it.
Check it with a pin meter and wait until two readings a week apart match [c3].
[b-roll: two meter readings, a week apart]

## Cause three: the top is held too tightly
Also, the base.
If the base is screwed tight across the width, the top cannot move, so it bends instead.
But if you use buttons or figure-eight fasteners in slots, the top slides as it moves [c4].
[close-up: a wooden button in its groove]

## The test
[split screen: straightedge on both faces]
Here is the test. Lay a straightedge across the top, then flip the top and lay it on the other face.
If the hollow faces the drier side of the room, it is cause one. If it moves with the season, it is cause three.
Readings that still drift by more than 2 points mean cause two.

## Takeaway
So a cupped top is not bad luck. It is water moving unevenly, or wood that is not allowed to move.
Next time we flatten this exact top by hand, without taking it apart. What would you try first?
