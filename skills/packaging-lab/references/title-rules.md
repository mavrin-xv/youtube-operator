# Title rules

## Hard limits (YouTube)

- 100 characters at most.
- No < or > characters.

## Working rules

- **Front-load.** The deciding words sit in the first 40 characters. Mobile cuts the title around 60 characters, so
  40 to 60 is a good draft target. Never pad to reach it.
- **Search videos**: the viewer's phrase near the start, the way they type it.
- **Browse videos**: curiosity plus a concrete object or number.
- **Concrete over abstract**: a number, a price, a measurement, a named object. "3 Fixes" beats "Some Tips".
- **Plain words, active voice.** No "ultimate guide", "nobody tells you", "you won't believe", no exclamation marks,
  no words in capitals for shouting.
- **Questions** have no built-in advantage. Use one when the question is exactly what the viewer types.
- **Title and thumbnail split the work.** For browse, never put the same words in both. For search, the thumbnail may
  repeat the key noun.
- **A formula slot that often fits**: subject + concrete result + mechanism ("Why Your Tabletop Cupped (and 3 Fixes
  That Work)").

## Title shapes worth trying

| Shape | Example |
|---|---|
| Why + felt problem | Why Your Box Joints Gap |
| Number + result | 3 Fixes for a Cupped Tabletop |
| I did X for Y | I Fixed a Cupped Walnut Top in 2 Days |
| Cost | What a $90 Workbench Gets Wrong |
| Versus with a verdict | Hide Glue vs PVA After 6 Months |
| Mistake | The Moisture Mistake Behind Cupped Tabletops |

Check which shapes over-index among your neighbours' outliers (the shape-lift table from `outlier_finder.py`), then
test them against each other. Shapes are hints, not rules.

## What the lint cannot see

Whether the promise is interesting, whether it is true, and whether it matches the thumbnail emotionally. Those are
the truth and stranger tests.
