# Thumbnail checklist (and A/B prep)

## File (YouTube limits)

- [ ] At least 1280 x 720, 16:9. Larger is fine (3840 x 2160 is common).
- [ ] Under 2 MB, JPG or PNG. `publish_lint.py --thumb file.png` checks size and ratio.

## Readability

- [ ] One focal point. At most three visual elements.
- [ ] High contrast between the subject and the background.
- [ ] Text 0 to 4 words, large enough to read at 120 px wide.
- [ ] Nothing important in the bottom-right corner (duration badge) or along the bottom edge (progress bar).
- [ ] Passes the phone test: 120 x 68 and 240 x 135, next to six real thumbnails from the same search, in light and
      dark mode.

## Promise

- [ ] Search video: 2 to 4 big words name the topic, plus one object that shows the subject.
- [ ] Browse video: the thumbnail tells the other half of the title (the loss, the question, the result) and does not
      repeat its words.
- [ ] The overlay text is ideally a line the video says, so the promise is paid by construction.
- [ ] Nothing fake: no invented result, chart or screenshot. Illustration looks like illustration.
- [ ] Consistent palette and type across the series so returning viewers recognise it.

## Evidence on what moves clicks (read with care)

- One drawn circle or arrow on the thing that matters showed a small lift in one large public study of breakout
  videos (Eden.so), and short text or none did best there. These are correlations from other channels.
- Faces looked neutral overall in a separate public analysis (1of10). A face is a choice to justify, not a rule.
- Extreme claims: push the premise to its true extreme, never past it. A mismatch makes viewers leave fast, and the
  retention cliff costs more than the extra clicks.
- The only evidence that counts for your channel is your own Test & Compare result.

## A/B prep for Test & Compare

- [ ] Variants differ in one idea, not in small details (a new concept beats a new colour).
- [ ] Each variant passes every check above on its own.
- [ ] Each variant is true to the video.
- [ ] Logged in the decision journal before the test starts: variants, hypothesis, the date to read the result.
