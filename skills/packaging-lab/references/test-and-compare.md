# Test & Compare (YouTube's built-in A/B test)

What the YouTube help pages say, as of writing (check them, features change):

- Test up to 3 thumbnails, 3 titles, or 3 title and thumbnail pairs on one long-form video.
- YouTube shows the variants to viewers and picks the winner by **watch time share**, not by CTR. That is the right
  metric: a variant that gets clicks but loses viewers fast should lose.
- A test can run up to two weeks. It can end as a winner, a "preferred" result, or inconclusive.
- It needs access to advanced features (a verified channel) and is available on desktop Studio.
- Editing the title or thumbnail while the test runs stops it.

Sources: YouTube Help "Test & compare thumbnails and titles" (support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714) and its
newer title testing page (support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400).

## How to use it well

1. Test concepts, not tweaks. Two thumbnails that differ only in colour will often end inconclusive.
2. Start at publish, or within the first 48 hours, when impressions are highest.
3. Do not change anything else on the video during the test (description, end screen, playlists).
4. Low-traffic videos often end inconclusive. That is a result: keep the variant you trust more.
5. Log the result in the decision journal: variants, winner, watch time shares, what you will reuse in the next
   package.

## Before you have the feature

Change one element at a time after publish, with a dated log entry, and compare the 7 days before with the 7 days
after on the same traffic source. Expect noise: under about 1,000 impressions per source, CTR moves by more than a
percentage point by chance alone.
